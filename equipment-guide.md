# Equipment Guide — @ilo.krafts TikTok
**Three budget levels. Start where you are. Upgrade when you can.**
All recommendations are for TikTok-first content creation.

---

## THE TRUTH ABOUT EQUIPMENT
The #1 thing that kills content quality is not equipment — it's bad lighting.
A $1,500 iPhone in bad light looks worse than a $400 Android in good light.
Prioritize lighting first. Camera second. Audio third. Everything else is optional.

---

## TIER 1 — STARTER ($0–$80)
*You have a modern smartphone. That's enough to start.*

### What You Have (Your Phone)
Any iPhone 13+ or Android flagship from the past 3 years is sufficient.
Set to: 1080p, 30fps, 9:16 vertical.

### What You Need to Add

**Lighting — $0 (Natural light hack)**
- Film within 3 feet of a window, with light hitting your FACE or the PRODUCT
- Time your filming session for 10 AM–2 PM when natural light is strongest
- Overcast days = soft, even light. Sunny days = harsh shadows (close the blinds slightly)
- Hang a white sheet in the window on very bright days to diffuse harsh sunlight

**Stabilization — $10–$25**
- Phone tripod: Any flexible/adjustable tripod from Amazon ($10–$15)
  - Look for: adjustable height, 360-degree rotation, phone grip that fits your phone size
  - Recommended search: "phone tripod 60 inch flexible"
- If on a zero budget: Stack books to set your phone at the right height. Lean it against a mug.

**Audio — $0 (Environment tricks)**
- Record in a small room with carpet, furniture, and curtains — all absorb echo
- A walk-in closet is genuinely the best free recording booth
- Film away from HVAC vents, fans, and refrigerators

**Editing — $0**
- CapCut (free, iOS + Android) — auto-captions, trending templates, full editor
- TikTok native editor (free, built into the app)

**Starter total: $10–$25**

## TIER 2 — GROWTH ($80–$300)
*You're posting consistently and ready to level up quality.*

### Lighting — $30–$60

**Option A: Ring Light**
- 10-inch or 12-inch LED ring light with phone holder
- Get one with: adjustable brightness (3 levels minimum), adjustable color temperature (warm/cool/daylight), height adjustment to 5+ feet
- Position: at eye level or slightly above, 2–3 feet from subject
- Best for: talking-head videos, product beauty shots
- Budget: $30–$50

**Option B: LED Panel Light**
- More natural-looking than ring lights (no circular catchlights)
- Better for: product videos, flat lays, tutorial setups
- Get: dimmable, 5600K daylight balanced, with diffuser panel
- Budget: $40–$80

**Pro tip:** Get TWO affordable lights ($20 each) rather than one expensive one. Two lights = fill light + key light = professional-looking depth.

**Reflector — $10–$15**
- 5-in-1 collapsible reflector (gold, silver, white, black, diffuser)
- Use the white side to bounce light back onto the shadow side of products
- Eliminates harsh one-sided shadows for almost nothing

### Audio — $20–$60

**Option A: Lavalier (Clip-on) Mic — $20–$35**
- Clip it 6 inches below your chin under your collar
- Best for: talking-head voiceovers, walking tours, LIVE sessions
- Look for: works with your phone's headphone jack or USB-C (check compatibility)
- Recommended: Boya BY-M1 or Rode SmartLav+ (if budget allows)

**Option B: Wireless Lavalier Mic — $40–$80**
- No cable = total freedom of movement
- Best for: filming yourself while demonstrating crafts, vendor spotlights
- Recommended: DJI Mic Mini (best for this price range), RODE Wireless GO II (premium)

**Directional (Shotgun) Mic — $25–$50**
- Mounts directly on your phone
- Captures sound in front, rejects background noise from sides
- Best for: stationary filming setups, voice-forward content
- Recommended: Rode VideoMicro, Movo VXR10

### Stabilization — $20–$80

**Tabletop tripod with arm — $20–$40**
- Extends arm over a flat surface = perfect for flat lay product shots, tutorial overhead shots
- Essential for: craft tutorials showing hands from above
- Look for: adjustable arm with 360-degree rotation

**Full-height tripod — $30–$60**
- For standing talking-head videos and product display shots
- Get: adjustable height up to 6 feet, 360-degree head rotation

**Overhead mount / gorilla pod — $15–$30**
- Flexible tripod that clamps to shelves, tables, ladders
- For creative angles that a regular tripod can't do

### Backdrop — $15–$40

**Paper backdrop roll — $20–$40**
- Seamless photography paper — clean, professional background with no creases
- Good for: product-only shoots, beauty shots
- Start with: white or light gray (most versatile)
- Mount to: a backdrop stand ($30) or tape to the wall and let it curve down to the table

**Fabric backdrop — $15–$25**
- Folds small, travels easily
- Slightly more texture than paper (some people prefer this for craft content)
- Options: muslin (natural), linen (textured), velvet (moody/luxury feel)

**Flat lay surfaces — $10–$30**
- Large marble contact paper sheets (apply to foam board for rigid, portable surface)
- Light wood grain boards
- Dark slate boards
- Buy 2–3 different ones — alternate for visual variety

**Tier 2 total: $80–$200 (selective upgrades)**

## TIER 3 — PROFESSIONAL ($300–$800)
*High volume posting, LIVE sessions, vendor-produced content.*

### Camera — $300–$800

**Option A: Stay on smartphone but upgrade to the latest flagship**
- iPhone 15 Pro / Samsung Galaxy S24 Ultra
- These genuinely shoot cinema-grade 4K
- No learning curve — you already know your phone

**Option B: Sony ZV-1 II / Canon G7 X Mark III — $500–$750**
- Compact vlog cameras with flip screen (you can see yourself while filming)
- Excellent autofocus, better low-light than most phones
- Shoots 4K, 1080p slow motion
- Most TikTok creators who use a camera use one of these two

**Option C: Sony ZV-E10 / Fujifilm X-T30 (mirrorless) — $600–$900**
- Interchangeable lenses = more creative flexibility
- Dramatically better image quality, especially in low light
- Steeper learning curve — only go here if you're serious about video quality long-term

**Best lens for product content:** 35mm f/1.8 prime (creates beautiful background blur on product shots)

### Lighting — $100–$300

**2-point softbox kit — $80–$150**
- Two softboxes create even, professional light with no harsh shadows
- Ideal for a dedicated filming corner in your space
- Look for: 45W CFL bulbs, adjustable stands, 24x24 inch softboxes minimum

**Aputure AL-MX / Aputure Amaran 100D — $150–$300**
- High-quality LED panel lights used by real video professionals
- Adjustable color temperature (2700K–6500K), adjustable brightness
- Built to last, consistent output — worth the investment if you film daily

**RGB LED panel — $50–$120**
- Full color range — lets you create dramatic colored backgrounds or accent lighting
- Good for: brand-colored backdrops, moody night-time aesthetics
- Use sparingly — works best as a secondary accent light, not primary

### Audio — $80–$200

**DJI Mic 2 — $130 (wireless lavalier)**
- Best wireless clip mic in this price range, hands down
- Magnetic clip attachment, 250m range, built-in safety track
- Works directly with phone, camera, or computer

**RODE VideoMic NTG — $150–$200 (shotgun)**
- Professional-grade directional mic
- USB-C and 3.5mm output — works with phones and cameras
- For high-quality voiceovers and vendor interview footage

### Editing Software — $0–$55/month

**Free:**
- CapCut (mobile + desktop) — all you need for TikTok
- DaVinci Resolve (desktop) — professional-grade color grading, free version is excellent

**Paid:**
- Adobe Premiere Pro ($55/month or Creative Cloud bundle)
- Final Cut Pro ($299 one-time, Mac only) — fastest professional editor for video creators

**LIVE session upgrade:**
- StreamYard or Streamlabs OBS (free–$25/month)
- Lets you display graphics, lower thirds, and product info during LIVE sessions
- Looks dramatically more professional than the default TikTok LIVE interface

**Tier 3 total: $300–$800 (built over time)**

## VENDOR-SIDE FILMING GUIDE
*Share this with vendors when you request content from them.*

### What to ask vendors to film:
1. **The making process** — hands at work, any part of the creation
2. **Raw materials** — the "before" shot
3. **Finished product beauty shots** — clean background, good light, multiple angles
4. **Their workspace** — gives context and personality
5. **Them holding or using the finished piece** — human element

### Minimum requirements for usable vendor content:
- Filmed on smartphone, horizontal or vertical (vertical preferred)
- Filmed in good natural light or near a window
- Audio not required (you'll add your own voiceover)
- At least 30 seconds of footage for each of the above shots
- No timestamps, watermarks, or other overlays

### What to send vendors to help them:
- Screenshot of this guide
- A voice note walking them through it takes 60 seconds
- A specific list of the 3–4 shots you need from them this week

## THE MINIMUM VIABLE SETUP
If you have zero budget and need to start TODAY:

1. Use your current phone
2. Stand near a window at 11 AM on an overcast day
3. Stack books to hold your phone at eye level
4. Record in your smallest room with carpet
5. Edit in CapCut (free)
6. Post

That's it. Start. Upgrade later.
